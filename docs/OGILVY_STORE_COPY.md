# Bayonne store — Ogilvy format (filled)

Ogilvy’s rules: the headline does 80% of the work, it states a fact rather than an adjective, and the body is long because people who are actually interested read. Nothing uses “elevate,” “premium,” or “iconic.” Specificity beats adjectives. Price belongs next to the ask.

## The body copy

> Garnet is a dark, slightly brown-toned red. Maroon is purple-toned. Burgundy is darker still. Put them side by side in a gym and you can see it from the top row.
>
> Most spirit wear is printed in whatever red the vendor already had loaded. It looks close on a screen and wrong in person, and it fades a shade further with every wash.
>
> We built this store so Bayonne parents stop settling for nearly the right red.
>
> **Things worth knowing before you order:**
>
> **1.** You see the name and number on the jersey before you pay. If the spelling is wrong, you catch it here — not in a box three weeks later.
>
> **2.** One matched garnet across the whole store. A crew bought in October matches a jersey ordered in April.
>
> **3.** Nothing is printed until you order it. There is no back room of unsold XLs.
>
> **4.** Adult sizes with a size chart in inches — chest and body length, not “runs small.”
>
> **5.** You design the piece here. Checkout finishes on a secure cart at [noparade-store.com](https://noparade-store.com).
>
> Cut until true in writing: print process brand-name (DTG vs AOP per blank), exact business-day ship window, booster/% split, free two-way returns, youth sizes, “every sport in the school.”

> The school has been at 669 Avenue A since 1936. Roughly none of the students, and none of their parents, should have to settle for a shirt that is nearly the right red.
